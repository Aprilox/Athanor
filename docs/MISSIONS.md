# Missions

*Français : [MISSIONS.fr.md](MISSIONS.fr.md)*

A mission is one sentence that several modules carry out together: **Studio** builds the project, **Servers** prepares where it runs. This page says what happens between your sentence and the lanes at work, what the orchestrator may and may not do, and what you approve. In the app, *Guide › Launch a mission* and *Guide › Follow a mission* walk through it; deployments themselves are in [DEPLOY.md](DEPLOY.md).

## From a sentence to a card

1. **The sentence.** In the *What do you want to do?* bar of the home page, for example “Make a site for the Martin bakery and put it online at martin.fr on @vps”. Typing `@` lists your projects, your servers and your vault entries **by name** (never a value); a choice becomes a chip. The *New project* chip under the bar asks for a new project. Athanor checks every mention again (an unknown one is refused) and keeps the sentence with any secret-looking text masked.
2. **The orchestrator.** *Send* creates the mission and starts the orchestrator; the card shows “The orchestrator is preparing the card…”. It reads the state of the modules and works out by itself what it can: a new project or one of yours, its name and folder, whether your sentence speaks of production (a live site, real users), the resources it will need. It asks only what it cannot guess, one question at a time, then proposes a card.
3. **The card.** What Studio will build (a new project and its folder, or one of yours) and the resources it uses (servers, vault keys, API providers) as chips. Only when a server is linked: what Servers will prepare (DNS, certificates, proxy, protection, firewall), the address where the site goes online, and a *Production* badge when your sentence speaks of production: it then always waits for your accord. *What will be touched* is folded. Environment names are internal (the address's host, or `site`): there is no list of environments to fill. A mission with no server looks like Studio on its own. Athanor checks the card before showing it: an existing project or a valid new name, servers from your inventory, addresses not used by another environment, vault entries that exist. An entry the vault does not hold is shown *to fill*; the orchestrator never creates one.

What the card shows next is **worked out by Athanor, not by the orchestrator**:

| Part | How |
|---|---|
| *I will ask for your approval for* | every risky action, whatever the server; production always; hardening a server not hardened yet; unlocking the vault when an *infra* entry is used |
| *What will be touched* | the servers, the DNS names and their zones, the files `/etc/caddy/sites/<host>.caddy` and `/srv/athanor/<project>-<env>/`, the project's folder |
| *Estimate* | the milestones the orchestrator expects × the average cycle time and quota of the projects on this PC (25 minutes and “quota unknown” before a first cycle is measured), shown as a range |

There is no policy to choose: one rule applies to every mission. Every risky action asks for your agreement, whatever the server, and so do destructive steps and anything on a server that Athanor cannot place (in doubt, caution; see the Servers agent's guardrails in the README), and it goes to the audit log. A deployment to a *Production* environment always waits for you, even if you ticked *Never ask again* for that server. The card's *I will ask for your approval for* list is worked out with these same rules, your *Never ask again* included: what it lists is what you will be asked.

If the orchestrator's session fails after showing you a card, the card stays and can still be launched; the error goes to the mission's history. While three missions are being prepared, a fourth waits: send it again once a card is ready. Sending the same sentence twice while it is being prepared gives the same mission.

## Edit, launch, cancel

- **Resources.** *Add a resource* offers *Server*, *Key* or *API / provider*, then a list with a search field, without what the card already has; the *×* of a chip takes it off. The card is worked out again at once, with no agent (approvals, what will be touched), and a card launched with an older version is refused. A server brings the Servers lane (in the card's environment, `site` if it has none); taking the last one off removes the lane and its access. A provider gives the vault entry of its token. A server is added or removed on the computer only; an unknown resource is refused and the card stays as it was.
- **Project.** For a new project, the folded *Project* section: the folder (*Choose…*; checked at once: a full path, its parent existing, never a system folder or another project's), the working mode (parallel, eco, fast; the framing and the time limit stay in *Manage*) and the GitHub repository (*No repository* by default, an existing one to link, or a new private one named after the project, made right after the project).
- **Edit.** *Edit* opens a field *Ask for a change*: write a change (“put the site on the home server”) and *Apply* sends the card and your words back to the orchestrator, which proposes a new one.
- **Launch.** Once only, under a lock, in this order: (1) the Studio lane: a new project is created (same checks as *New project*) with the Studio brief as its request, or the brief is added to an existing project's queue; its pilot starts; (2) the ticked entries are given (*code* entries to the project, *infra* entries to the environment); (3) the environments are set (a *Production* one always on approval; the environment that is not *Production* is deployed after each milestone); (4) the Servers lane waits for the project's plan: as soon as `.athanor/PLAN.md` exists (end of the framing), one task prepares the server from a standard list fitted to the plan (packages, web server, user, folder, certificate, firewall), once only, its plans waiting for your approval as usual, while Studio builds; the deployment then only adds the delivery. A step that fails stops the following ones, rings the bell and is written in the mission's history; what is already done stays done, and *Resume the launch* goes on from the first step not done (the project and the repository are never created twice). If an *infra* entry is used, the vault must be unlocked first. In native Windows mode the Servers lane and *infra* entries still run: there is no barrier, so the card says that the servers' keys can be read by the mission's agents, and launching counts as *Force* for the project (written in the audit log, like the Servers lane launched without a barrier). On the computer, *Launch* asks for Athanor's password, since it gives keys. The new project's folder is checked before anything starts (a full local path, its parent writable, not taken). An existing project's environments are never weakened: their approval policy never gets looser, and their protection and servers stay as they are unless you change them yourself; the card shows “Before:” under each environment the mission changes. From a phone, environments and the project are changed on the computer only.
- **Cancel.** Before launching, *Cancel the mission* drops it: nothing has been built or deployed.

The *Missions* page lists every mission with its state and its lanes (read live from the project's pilot and the Servers task); a mission's page shows its card and links to its project and its task. The last three are on the home page.
- **After the launch.** Each delivered milestone is deployed to the environment that is not *Production*, then checked from this PC. Once the Studio lane finished the request, the latest checked deployment carries the project's latest version and the Servers tasks of the *Production* environment are done, Athanor asks you "Deploy <host> to production?" (bell, *For you*, a notification that opens the mission). That accord is for this mission, this exact version and these servers, once, always, whatever the server; if the card or the version changed meanwhile, it is refused and asked again. Accepted: the *Production* environment is deployed then checked, and the mission ends. Refused: the mission waits for your *Ask again*. A failure opens the usual incident (*Retry*, *Go back to the previous version*, *I take over*), written in the mission's journal. Without a *Production* environment on the card, the mission ends as soon as the milestone deployment is checked.
- **Stop and resume.** *Stop* offers three ways. *Finish the current milestone*: the pilot completes and saves it, then the mission pauses (its state reads *Stopping after the milestone* meanwhile). *Put on pause*: the project's pilot stops now and the mission is held: no pilot start, no deployment after a milestone, no Servers plan step and no production accord until you *Resume*. *Stop immediately*: its pilot stops, its Servers preparations stop, its agents' sessions are revoked, its pending accords are refused and nothing is deployed after each milestone any more. A paused, stopping or stopped mission keeps *Resume* (Athanor's password asked): the pilot starts again, the suspended Servers preparations start once, the milestone deployment comes back and a refused production accord is asked again; nothing is replayed. Nothing is undone: the mission's journal lists what stays (environments, access given, files on the servers). The emergency stop pauses every launched mission.
- **When Athanor restarts.** Launched and paused missions carry on where they were: nothing is launched again and no access is given twice; the journal says “Athanor restarted”. An accord still pending for a card the mission no longer has is refused. A mission that needs your servers' keys waits for the vault to be unlocked (bell, journal), then goes on by itself. A mission whose card was being prepared gets its card back if it had one; otherwise it fails with the reason: send it again.
- **Delete.** *Delete the mission* (the *×* of its line on the *Missions* page, or the button of its page) says first what goes: the mission, its journal, its Servers tasks and its follow-up files. A running mission asks how to stop: *Finish the current milestone, then delete it* (ticked; deleted once the milestone is saved, also after a restart; *Resume* cancels it) or *Stop it immediately, then delete it*. Two boxes, unticked: *Also remove the project from Studio and send its folder to the recycle bin*, only for a project this mission created, used by no other mission, whose folder is a real folder (never a link, a junction, a drive, your personal or a system folder; otherwise the box is greyed with the reason), and *Also remove the access it gave* (the vault entries stay). The GitHub repository stays. For each environment where the mission prepared or deployed something, an unticked box *Also remove the site from the servers* (name, address, servers): it asks Athanor's password again and launches a Servers task that proposes the removal (Compose project, deployment folder, Caddy site, deploy key, what served this site alone, the DNS record if its provider can be reached); you accept its plan in *Servers*. Never the server itself, its keys nor another site. *Clear finished missions* deletes the finished, cancelled, failed and stopped ones (their projects and folders stay). Every deletion is written in the audit log.

## Following a mission

A launched mission has its own page, `#missions/<id>` (from the *Missions* page or a notification). It reads the mission again every 2 seconds while it is shown.

- **Header.** The sentence, the state, *Resume* when the mission is paused, stopping or stopped, and *Stop* (its three ways, each said in one sentence; *Cancel* has the focus).
- **Lanes.** Studio and Servers side by side (one column on a phone): the project with “Milestone 3/8: contact form” and what its pilot is doing; the Servers task with its plan step, or *See the plan* when it waits for you. Each name opens its page.
- **Relay.** *Milestone delivered*, *Going online*, *Verification*, then *Your accord* and *Production* when the card has a *Production* badge: the done ones ticked, the current one animated, a failed one red; the addresses below (*Online:* and *Production:*) once they exist. When the production accord was refused or expired, *Ask again* asks once more.
- **Journal.** One line per event in plain words, newest first, with its time (a milestone delivered, a deployment checked from outside, the accord asked, given or refused, a pause, a restart); warnings in amber, errors in red; 50 lines, then *See more*. No secret value is ever written there.
- **Incident.** When the mission's site is found unwell, its incident comes first on the page with the bell's actions: *Retry*, *Go back to the previous version*, *I take over*.
- **The production accord.** “Deploy <host> to production?” shows the address, the short commit, the version online now and what changes (the number of new commits and the subjects of the last three, any vault value masked). On a paired, unlocked phone, *Accept* is enough; a phone not paired or locked is refused; on the computer, Athanor's password is asked. Accepting twice has no effect: it is deployed once.
- **In a terminal.** *E* lists the missions with their state and relay step; *Enter* opens one: its lanes, its relay as text (✓ done, ● in progress, ✗ failed, ○ to come) and the 10 newest journal lines; *p* pauses, *r* resumes with the password, *a* stops after a confirmation, *d* deletes the mission (its own files only; a running one is stopped immediately first). The production accord is decided on page *6* (*For you*).

**Tested end to end.** `TestMissionP5`, in the `servers-ssh` CI job on a test SSH container, runs a whole mission: the sentence, the card, *Launch*, a milestone deployed and checked on a protected test environment (401 without the password, 200 with it), Athanor restarted in the middle, the production accord from a paired phone, the *Production* environment deployed and checked, then *Go back* on the test environment; the journal has no duplicate and no secret value. `TestMissionRedemarrage` covers the restart, `TestMissionP7` the phone accord and `TestRedTeamMissions` hostile agents.

## From your phone

A paired, unlocked phone can read a mission, answer the orchestrator, edit, launch, cancel, pause, resume and stop. When the orchestrator asks you to launch, “Launch the mission …?” comes in the bell, on *For you* and as a notification: it shows the card's environments (a *Production* one included) and the ticked access, and its link opens the mission. Accepting launches it exactly as *Launch* does, and only the card it was asked for: while it waits, neither the orchestrator nor *Edit* can change the card, and a card changed anyway refuses the request (launch it again from the card). On the computer, accepting asks for Athanor's password. Such an agreement is never “always”. The boxes you untick stay unticked when the orchestrator proposes a new card. The orchestrator's questions come in the bell too, with their choices as buttons; your answer restarts it where it stopped.

## What the orchestrator can and cannot do

The orchestrator is a short Claude session started by Athanor: an empty working folder, no built-in tool (`--tools ""`), only Athanor's MCP server, no secret in its environment, a token valid for this mission only and revoked when it stops, ten minutes at most. Its tools:

| Tool | What it does |
|---|---|
| `modules_etat` | a summary of the modules: projects with their state and their plan in one line, servers with their name, state and hardening, environments with their address and protection, names and types of the vault entries — never a value or a file |
| `proposer_mission` | hands over the card; Athanor checks it and works out the rest, or answers with what to correct |
| `demander_module` | a read-only question to a module, from an allow-list (today: a project's state) |
| `question_proprietaire` | asks you a question with up to 4 choices; one open question at a time |
| `lancer_mission` | asks you to launch (the approval above); it never launches by itself |

Every other tool — Studio's, the Servers agent's, Cloudflare's, files, SSH — is refused to its token, and each call is written to the audit log. It cannot read a project's files, reach a server, see a key, build or deploy: once launched, the work is done by the project's pilot and the Servers agent, under their usual rules.

## Where it is kept

One file per mission in Athanor's folder, `hub/missions/<id>.json` (readable by your account only): the sentence, the mentions, the card, the lanes, the questions and the history. The orchestrator's workshop (`hub/missions/<id>-session/`) is deleted when its session ends.
